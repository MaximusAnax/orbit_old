# **Core Product Thesis**

The platform is fundamentally:

> **A private AI system that remembers your relationships better than you can — and helps you sustain and activate them meaningfully over time.**
> 

This is not:

- social media,
- a CRM,
- or merely a networking app.

It is best described as:

> **relationship intelligence software.**
> 

---

# **Why Existing Solutions Fail**

## **LinkedIn**

- public-facing rather than personal,
- weak memory/context layer,
- relationship depth not captured,
- too noisy and performative,
- poor for private notes and emotional continuity.

---

## **Phone Contacts**

- no context,
- no semantic search,
- no relationship history,
- no maintenance support.

---

## **Spreadsheets / Notion CRMs**

- too manual,
- too transactional,
- habit formation weak,
- emotionally sterile.

---

## **Social Media**

- fragmented across platforms,
- not organized around relationship memory,
- optimized for broadcasting, not continuity.

---

# **What Makes This Product Unique**

## **1. It Solves a Cognitive Problem, Not Just an Organizational One**

The product is fundamentally about:

- reducing cognitive load,
- offloading social memory,
- and helping users think relationally at scale.

This is deeper than contact management.

---

## **2. It Blends Utility With Emotional Warmth**

Most products choose:

- optimization OR humanity.

This product intentionally combines:

- strategic utility,
- with emotional reassurance.

It should feel:

- useful,
- calming,
- intelligent,
- and thoughtful simultaneously.

---

## **3. Natural Language as the Core Interface**

The product avoids rigid data entry whenever possible.

Users should interact naturally:

Example:

> **“Met Sarah at TartanHacks. She’s doing ML infra at Databricks and mentioned recruiting opens in August.”**
> 

The AI transforms this into structured relational memory.

This dramatically reduces friction.

---

## **4. It Turns Hidden Social Value Into Visible Insight**

A key “wow” moment is:

> **realizing your network is richer than you thought.**
> 

The system reveals:

- clusters,
- opportunities,
- hidden connections,
- relationship patterns,
- and rediscovered people.

This creates both excitement and relief.

---

# **Core Product Features (MVP)**

## **1. Fast Relationship Capture**

The most important feature.

Users must be able to add someone in under 30–60 seconds.

Methods:

- typed notes,
- voice input,
- contact import,
- business card scan,
- social/profile links.

AI extracts:

- name,
- role,
- company,
- context,
- interests,
- follow-up ideas,
- tags,
- meeting source.

---

## **2. Intelligent Profiles**

Each person becomes a living relationship record.

Including:

- summary,
- timeline,
- notes,
- interaction history,
- reminders,
- relationship strength,
- shared context.

---

## **3. Natural Language Search**

Critical feature.

Examples:

- “Who do I know in robotics?”
- “People I met through hackathons.”
- “Who works in climate tech?”
- “People at OpenAI.”

The system acts as semantic memory retrieval.

---

## **4. Follow-Up Engine**

Creates retention and long-term value.

Examples:

- reconnect suggestions,
- life event prompts,
- recruiting reminders,
- contextual outreach timing.

Tone should remain:

- warm,
- supportive,
- non-judgmental.

Never guilt-inducing.

---

## **5. Opportunity Mode**

User enters a goal:

> **“Looking for ML internships in SF.”**
> 

The system surfaces:

- relevant people,
- intro pathways,
- dormant relationships,
- suggested outreach.

This is the bridge between Identity A and Identity B.

---

# **What NOT to Build Initially**

## **1. Graph-First Interface**

The original graph concept was emotionally insightful but operationally premature.

The graph was attempting to solve:

> **“How do users feel the richness of their network?”**
> 

However:

- graph UIs are expensive,
- difficult,
- and often low-frequency usage.

The graph may eventually become:

- exploratory mode,
- cluster visualization,
- or opportunity mapping.

But not the core interaction model.

---

## **2. Social Network Features**

This should remain:

> **a private intelligence system.**
> 

Not another public social platform.

---

## **3. Massive Scraping Infrastructure**

Avoid overcommitting to:

- web scraping,
- LinkedIn replication,
- autonomous data maintenance.

Focus first on:

- user-entered intelligence,
- semantic organization,
- and AI-assisted recall.

---

# **User Experience Philosophy**

The product should not feel like:

- work,
- admin,
- or relationship optimization software.

It should feel like:

- relief,
- continuity,
- support,
- social clarity.

The emotional target:

> **“I no longer have to carry all of this in my head.”**
> 

---

# **Onboarding Philosophy**

Do NOT onboard users into features.

Onboard them into:

> **emotional relief.**
> 

Potential onboarding prompt:

> **“Who’s someone you don’t want to lose touch with?”**
> 

This immediately humanizes the experience.

---

# **The Long-Term Vision**

The long-term vision is not merely helping users:

- store contacts,
- or optimize networking.

The deeper vision is:

> **helping humans sustain richer, more intentional relationships in an era where social scale exceeds biological memory.**

- Facebook expanded the reach of human networks.

- reducing relational entropy,
- preserving social continuity,
